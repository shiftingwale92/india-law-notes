The Act and the DPDP Rules, 2025 are **notified**, but both commence in stages.

So **as of 28 October 2026 the Act's core duties, rights and penalties and Rules 3, 5-16, 22-23 are notified but not in force**. The Data Protection Board of India has been established. Businesses are expected to use this runway to prepare; that preparation is what the compliance-workflows repo covers.
## 2. Who and what it covers (ss. 2-3)
- **Personal data**: any data about an individual who is identifiable by or in relation to it (s.2(t)).
- **Data Principal**: the individual the data is about; includes parents/guardians for children and persons with disability (s.2(j)).
- **Data Fiduciary**: whoever determines the purpose and means of processing (s.2(i)).
- **Data Processor**: processes on behalf of a Data Fiduciary (s.2(k)).
- **Consent Manager**: a person registered with the Board who acts as a single point of contact for giving, managing, reviewing and withdrawing consent (s.2(g)).
- Applies to digital personal data processed in India (collected digitally or digitised later), and to processing outside India connected with offering goods or services to Data Principals in India (s.3).

## 3. Consent and notice (ss. 5-6)
Processing generally needs consent, which must be free, specific, informed, unconditional and unambiguous, given by clear affirmative action, and limited to the necessary data (s.6). Every consent request must be accompanied or preceded by a **notice** describing the personal data and purpose, how to exercise rights and withdraw consent, and how to complain to the Board (s.5). Consent can be withdrawn as easily as it was given. The Act also lists "certain legitimate uses" (s.7) where consent is not needed, such as voluntary provision for a specified purpose, State functions, medical emergency and employment purposes. Under s.6(9) a Data Principal may give, manage, review and withdraw consent through a **Consent Manager** - a person registered with the Board who acts as a single point of contact over an interoperable platform. The Consent Manager provisions (s.6(9) and Rule 4 with its First Schedule conditions) commence on the computed date **13 November 2026** (one year after Gazette publication, per the notification's own interval); they are not operative today.

## 4. Data Fiduciary obligations (s. 8)
- Overall responsibility for compliance, including for processing by a Data Processor.
- **Reasonable security safeguards** to prevent breach (s.8(5)).
- **Breach intimation**: on any personal data breach, intimate the Board **and each affected Data Principal** "in such form and manner as may be prescribed" (s.8(6)). Rule 7 of the DPDP Rules, 2025 (not yet in force; computed commencement 13 May 2027) details two limbs: (a) to each affected Data Principal, without delay, in clear and plain terms - description, likely consequences, mitigation, safety measures and a contact; (b) to the Board, without delay, with detailed particulars following within **72 hours** (or a longer period the Board allows on a written request). The Rule prescribes the content and timing of the intimations; it does not prescribe a fixed form. Unlike the GDPR there is no harm threshold; every breach is reportable.
- **Independent, in-force regime**: CERT-In's directions of 28 April 2022 (No. 20(3)/2022-CERT-In, under s.70B(6) IT Act) already require service providers, intermediaries, data centres, body corporates and government organisations to report listed cyber incidents - including data breaches and data leaks - to CERT-In within **6 hours** of noticing them. This applies today, independent of the DPDP timeline. Source: https://www.cert-in.org.in/PDF/CERT-In_Directions_70B_28.04.2022.pdf
- Erase personal data when consent is withdrawn or the specified purpose is no longer served, subject to legal retention requirements (s.8(7)).

## 5. Children (s. 9)
Verifiable consent of parent or lawful guardian before processing a child's data. No behavioural monitoring or targeted advertising directed at children, and no processing detrimental to a child's well-being.

## 6. Significant Data Fiduciaries (s. 10)
Entities notified by the Central Government based on factors like volume and sensitivity of data must also: appoint an India-based **Data Protection Officer** answerable to the board of directors, appoint an **independent data auditor**, and conduct periodic **Data Protection Impact Assessments** and audits.

## 7. Rights and duties of Data Principals (ss. 11-15)
Rights: access to information about processing (s.11); correction, completion, updating and erasure (s.12); readily available grievance redressal (s.13); nomination (s.14). Duties (s.15) include not impersonating, not suppressing material information, and not filing false or frivolous complaints.

## 8. Regulator, enforcement and penalties
- **Data Protection Board of India**: inquires on breach intimations under s.8(6), Data Principal complaints, references from the Central or State Government, court directions, and complaints or intimations about Consent Managers (s.27(1)) - the Act lists no suo motu trigger. Functions as a digital office.
- **Appeals**: Telecom Disputes Settlement and Appellate Tribunal (TDSAT).
- **Penalties** (Schedule, see s.33(1); stated as maximum amounts - 'may extend to'):
  - Failure to take reasonable security safeguards (s.8(5)): up to Rs 250 crore
  - Failure to notify Board / affected Data Principals of a breach (s.8(6)): up to Rs 200 crore
  - Breach of children's-data obligations (s.9): up to Rs 200 crore
  - Breach of Significant Data Fiduciary obligations (s.10): up to Rs 150 crore
  - Breach of Data Principal duties (s.15): up to Rs 10,000
  - Any other breach of the Act or Rules: up to Rs 50 crore
- The Schedule takes effect with s.33 at stage (c), computed 13 May 2027; penalties are not imposable under the DPDP Act today.

## 9. Practical starting points for a small organisation
1. Map what personal data you hold and why (see compliance-workflows repo, data inventory).
2. Rewrite collection points around notice + consent (or document the s.7 basis).
3. Set up a breach register and drill both clocks: CERT-In's 6-hour reporting window for covered entities (in force today) and the DPDP 72-hour Board particulars track once operative.
4. Publish a grievance channel and track rights requests.
5. Review vendor contracts for processor obligations.
